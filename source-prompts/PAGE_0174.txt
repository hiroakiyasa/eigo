Top Japanese title: 「dare と助動詞で強く言う」

Top panel: dare means having the courage to do something; it is usually easiest as an ordinary verb. Show:
• She didn't dare to open the box. 「箱を開ける勇気がなかった」
• Do you dare to cross the bridge? 「渡る勇気はある？」
Add expressive patterns:
• How dare you touch my notes! 「よくも私のノートに触ったな」
• Try it if you dare. 「できるものならやってみて」
Small note: modal-like need/dare occur mainly in questions and negatives in some varieties, but learners can safely use ordinary-verb patterns: don't need to / do you need to / don't dare to.

Bottom mechanism: an audio-volume console. Strong stress on an auxiliary or be verb emphasizes the entire claim:
• I WILL finish today. 「絶対今日終える」
• You CAN solve it. 「君なら本当にできる」
• I AM the guide. 「私がガイドです！」
If there is no auxiliary, insert emphatic do/does/did + base verb:
• I DO trust you. / She DOES remember. / We DID call.

Mini practice: 「『本当に彼に電話した』を強調: I ___ call him.」 Answer: did. Reason: 「過去の一般動詞は DID + 原形で強調」.
