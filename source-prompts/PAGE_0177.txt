Top Japanese title: 「can と be able to の使い分け」

Central split mechanism:
CAN/COULD = potential ability, what someone generally can/could do.
BE ABLE TO = factual ability and flexible grammar; in the past, it can highlight one achieved success.

Original examples with concise Japanese:
• I can read music. 「楽譜を読める（一般的能力）」
• My aunt could repair old radios. 「昔その能力があった」
• After two hours, we were able to unlock the door. 「実際に解錠できた」
• I want to be able to speak confidently. 「to不定詞の中では be able to」

Add emphasis note: can is normally the natural short choice for ordinary present ability; be able to may highlight achievement or fit positions where a modal cannot appear.

Show the one-modal rule with nesting blocks:
• Maya may be able to join us. ✓
• Maya may can join us. ✗
• You will be able to download it later. ✓

Small urgent-advice badge: had better + base verb means 「そうしないと困る」, stronger/more urgent than casual advice. Example “You'd better save your work.” Negative: “You'd better not touch that wire.”

Mini practice: 「一度の成功『ついに橋を渡れた』: We finally ___ cross the bridge.」 Answer: were able to. Reason: 「過去の具体的な達成」.

Takeaway: 「能力なら can、形の自由や実際の達成なら be able to」. Dense but clear.
