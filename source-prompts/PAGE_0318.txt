Top Japanese title: 「代用で繰り返しを軽くする」

Central before/after mechanism: a heavy sentence repeats the same noun or action; a small token replaces it while meaning remains connected. Core rule: 「一度出た内容を短い形で受け、文章を滑らかにする」.

Noun substitution:
ONE / ONES replace countable nouns.
• This lantern is dim. Give me a brighter one. 「a brighter lantern」
• The wooden boxes are heavy; the plastic ones are lighter. 「plastic boxes」
Add caution: uncountable nouns are not normally replaced by one in the same way.

Verb-phrase substitution ladder:
• “Will you label every bag?” — “I’ll do so now.” 「前の行為を丁寧に丸ごと受ける」
• Rina checked the map, and I did it too. 「くだけた具体的行為」
• Don’t move the marker. Ken did that yesterday. 「その行為を指して受ける」
• I rarely cook, but my brother does. 「doesだけでcookを機械的に代用」
Explain nuance: do so is explicit/formal; do it/that is more conversational; bare do/does/did is the leanest grammatical substitute.
