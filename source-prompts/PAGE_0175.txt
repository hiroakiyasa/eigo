Top Japanese title: 「have to は必要性から進む」

Central illustration: an external-reason engine—rules, schedule, evidence—pushes a cart labelled HAVE TO along a necessary path. Contrast briefly: must often foregrounds the speaker's strong judgment; have to foregrounds practical or external necessity.

Two main uses with original examples:

Obligation/necessity:
• I have to renew my passport this week. 「今週更新する必要がある」
• She has to wear safety glasses at work. 「職場の規則・事情」
• We had to change trains. 「過去の必要」

Strong conclusion from evidence:
• These footprints have to belong to a large dog. 「この足跡は大型犬のものに違いない」
• This has to be the correct entrance. 「証拠から見てここに違いない」
