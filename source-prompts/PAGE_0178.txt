Top Japanese title: 「used to と would の過去習慣」

Central illustration: a clear NOW/THEN contrast bridge. USED TO + base verb describes a past habit or state with an implied contrast: now it is different. WOULD + base verb recalls repeated past actions, often within a story; it normally does not describe a past state by itself.

Original examples with Japanese:
• I used to live beside the sea. 「以前は海辺に住んでいた（今は違う）」
• There used to be a bakery here. 「以前ここにパン屋があった」
• Every summer, we would sleep under the stars. 「夏になるとよく星空の下で寝た」
• My grandfather would hum while he cooked. 「料理中いつも鼻歌を歌った」

Show contrast table:
past state: used to ✓ / would usually ✗
repeated past action: both possible, but would needs a past-time context and feels like recollection.

Form strip: Did you use to...? / didn't use to...; pronunciation note “used to” often sounds like /juːstə/.

Mini practice: choose used to/would: “There ___ be a pond behind this school.” Answer: used to. Reason: 「過去の状態＋現在との対比」.
