Top Japanese title: 「否定倒置とifなし条件文」

Upper mechanism: ordinary sentence becomes emphatic in two moves.
1 move a negative/restrictive phrase to the front
2 invert auxiliary and subject
• I had never heard such a clear echo. → Never had I heard such a clear echo! 「Neverを強打し、had Iが呼応」
• We understood the clue only after sunrise. → Only after sunrise did we understand the clue. 「その時になって初めて」
• No sooner had the gate opened than the crowd rushed in. 「門が開くとすぐ群衆が入った」

Add a balance-scale illustration: “Never I had...” feels structurally unbalanced; “Never had I...” matches fronted emphasis with inversion. Label the ungrammatical form clearly with a red X.

Lower mechanism: formal conditional inversion. Show IF tile removed while HAD or WERE moves to the gate front.
• If we had left later, we would have missed the ferry. → Had we left later, we would have missed the ferry. 「もし遅く出ていたら」
• If I were in charge, I would simplify the plan. → Were I in charge, I would simplify the plan. 「仮に責任者なら」
• Were it not for this compass, we would be lost. 「このコンパスがなければ」
Caption: 「Had/Wereの倒置そのものが条件を示すのでifは置かない」. Note: polished, formal, often written style.

Mini practice: 「If she had noticed the sign, ... を倒置」 Answer: 「Had she noticed the sign, ...」 Reason: 「ifを外し、hadを主語sheの前へ出す」.

Bottom takeaway: 「否定は強調の倒置、Had/Wereは条件の倒置」. Dense but orderly and readable.
