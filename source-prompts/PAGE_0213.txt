Top Japanese title: 「カンマは注釈の合図」

Central contrast: no comma = identifies which person/thing; commas = adds optional information to something already identified. Show a narrowing spotlight versus a detachable side-note bubble.

Original pairs and examples:
• The scientist who built this robot lives nearby. 「どの科学者かを限定」
• Dr. Mina, who built this robot, lives nearby. 「Minaへの補足」
• We stayed at Star Harbor, which Mia recommended. 「固有名詞への追加情報」
• Leo, whose compass was broken, used the stars. 「所有関係を含む注釈」

Rule badge: comma relative clauses use who / which / whose, not that; removing the comma clause should leave the main identification intact.

Mini practice: correct “Kyoto, that I visited last spring, is beautiful.” Answer: “Kyoto, which I visited last spring, is beautiful.” Reason: 「カンマ付き注釈ではthatを使わない」.
